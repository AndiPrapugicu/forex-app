# Event playbook

Background on what each release is and the **usual** first reaction. The dossier's section E, where present, has what THIS market actually did on the last few prints. That record beats this file.

The reaction is to the **surprise** (actual against forecast), not the level. The same surprise can move markets differently depending on what the Fed is worried about. When inflation is the worry, hot prices matter most; when jobs are the worry, weak payrolls matter most.

## US releases (times in New York; UTC is +4h in US summer time, +5h in winter)
- **CPI** (BLS, 08:30 monthly): hot → yields and the dollar up, gold and indices down (Nasdaq most). Core matters more than headline. Soft → the reverse.
- **Employment Situation / NFP** (BLS, first Friday, 08:30): payrolls, unemployment rate and average hourly earnings arrive together. Strong jobs plus hot wages is hawkish; weak payrolls plus a rising unemployment rate is dovish. When the three disagree, wages and the unemployment rate often decide the second move.
- **PCE prices** (BEA, monthly, 08:30): the Fed's preferred inflation gauge. Smaller moves than CPI, because CPI already hinted at it.
- **ISM PMIs** (ISM, 10:00 on the first business days): growth signals. The prices-paid sub-index matters for inflation.
- **Retail sales, GDP, jobless claims** (08:30): growth signals. A strong print is good for stocks unless the Fed is the worry ("good news is bad news").
- **Michigan sentiment** (10:00): small moves, except its inflation-expectations line.

## The Fed
- **FOMC decision** (14:00) and **press conference** (14:30): the statement, the dot plot (quarterly) and the chair's tone. The first move often reverses during the press conference.
- **Minutes** (14:00, three weeks after a meeting): move markets when they show a different balance of views than the statement.
- **Fed speakers**: matter when they move the expected path, which fed funds futures show. Rhetoric the futures do not reprice is noise.

## Bonds and oil
- **Treasury coupon auctions** close at 13:00. Strong demand pulls yields down, weak demand (a tail) pushes them up; long-bond auctions move the long end most.
- **EIA weekly petroleum status** (Wednesday 10:30): a crude stock draw larger than expected is bullish oil, a build bearish.
- **OPEC+ meetings**: cuts or extended cuts are bullish oil; unwinding cuts is bearish.

## Regimes that flip the sign
- **"Good news is bad news"**: when inflation is the worry, strong data lifts yields and hurts stocks.
- **"Bad news is bad news"**: when recession is the worry, weak data hurts stocks even as yields fall.
- The dossier's market state (section 0) and the latest Fed texts say which regime applies now.
