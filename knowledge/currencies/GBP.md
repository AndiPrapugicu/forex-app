# British pound (GBP)

## Central bank: the Bank of England
- **Mandate.** Keep inflation at **2%** over the medium term. The target is set by the Government. (BoE, monetary policy)
- **Who decides.** The **Monetary Policy Committee**: nine members, each voting individually. The Governor has the casting vote if needed. **The vote split is information.** A 6–3 hold with three votes for a hike is a very different signal from a 9–0 hold. (BoE)
- **Instrument.** **Bank Rate**, the rate paid on overnight reserves. (BoE)
- **Meetings.** Eight a year, roughly every six weeks. As of 16 September 2026 Bank Rate was **3.75%**, with the next decision due **5 November 2026**. (BoE monetary policy page)
- **Lags.** The BoE says policy takes about **18–24 months** to have its full effect, so it reacts to the inflation outlook rather than to the latest print. (BoE)

## Economy: an energy and capital importer
- **Energy.** UK net import dependency was **43.3% in 2025** and **47.1% in Q1 2026**. The UK has been a net importer of natural gas since 2004. (DESNZ Energy Trends, March 2026 edition)
- **Current account.** The UK has run an **annual current account deficit every year since 1984**. The underlying deficit, excluding precious metals, was **1.4% of GDP in Q2 2026**. The ONS notes that higher energy prices risk widening the trade deficit in 2026. (ONS, balance of payments)
  - *Implication (background):* a persistent current-account deficit means sterling depends on foreign capital inflows. That makes it sensitive to confidence in UK fiscal policy and to global risk appetite.

## A dated episode: the gilt crisis
- **28 September 2022.** After a sharp, disorderly rise in long-dated gilt yields exposed leveraged liability-driven investment (LDI) pension funds, the BoE bought long-dated gilts to restore orderly markets. It purchased **£19.3bn** in all, between 28 September and 14 October. (BoE news and market notices, September–October 2022)
  - *Lesson (background):* when UK fiscal credibility is questioned, gilts and sterling can fall **together**. That is a risk-premium move, the opposite of the usual "higher yields lift the currency" channel.

## Channels that move sterling (background)
1. **BoE vs Fed and ECB path**, and the MPC vote split.
2. **UK services inflation and wage growth**, which the MPC watches closely.
3. **Energy prices** (UK gas tracks the European hub) through inflation and the trade balance.
4. **Fiscal credibility**: budgets and gilt auctions.
5. **Risk appetite**: sterling tends to behave as a pro-cyclical currency.

## How to read GBP in a pair
GBP is the base of GBPUSD, GBPJPY, GBPCHF, GBPAUD and the other sterling crosses, and the quote of EURGBP.
