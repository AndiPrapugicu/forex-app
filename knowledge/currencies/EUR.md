# Euro (EUR)

## Central bank: the European Central Bank
- **Mandate.** Price stability, defined as **2% inflation over the medium term**, symmetric. The mandate comes from Article 127(1) of the EU Treaty. Supporting wider EU objectives is secondary to it. (ECB, monetary policy intro)
- **Who decides.** The Governing Council: the six Executive Board members plus the governors of the euro area national central banks. It sets policy rates **every six weeks**. (ECB)
- **Instrument.** The **deposit facility rate (DFR) is the rate that steers the stance**. This was confirmed in March 2024, when the spread between the main refinancing rate and the DFR was cut to **15 basis points** (in effect from 18 September 2024). Calendars often headline the refinancing rate, which sits 15bp above the DFR. (ECB press release, 13 March 2024)
- **Fragmentation backstop.** The **Transmission Protection Instrument** (21 July 2022) lets the Eurosystem buy a member state's bonds when its financing conditions worsen in a way its fundamentals do not justify, subject to fiscal criteria. Spread blow-outs, for example in Italy or France, are therefore partly capped, though not removed as a risk to the euro. (ECB press release, 21 July 2022)

## Economy: an energy importer
- **Import dependency.** The EU met **58.4% of its energy needs through net imports in 2023 and 57% in 2024**. (Eurostat news, March 2026)
- **Natural gas.** Preliminary 2025 data show **88% of the gas supply in the grid came from imports**. (Eurostat, Energy production and imports, updated June 2026)
- **What an energy shock does.** In 2021–22, surging energy prices **worsened the euro area's terms of trade**, transferring purchasing power abroad. The ECB estimated the transfer at around **1.3% of GDP in Q4 2021**. The current account swung from a **+2.8% of GDP surplus in 2021 to a −0.8% deficit in 2022**, the largest annual change on record. The energy trade deficit reached **4.0% of GDP** in 2022. (ECB Economic Bulletin, box 2022/3 and article 2023/6)
  - *Implication (background):* rising TTF gas and Brent prices are a headwind for the euro through the trade balance and real incomes. They can also force the ECB to choose between fighting imported inflation and protecting growth. Read the cross-asset section for TTF and Brent, and the ECB texts for how the Council weighs the two.

## Channels that move the euro (background)
1. **ECB vs Fed path.** The DFR against fed funds, and the 2-year Germany/euro-area AAA yield against US Treasuries.
2. **Terms of trade.** Energy prices, as above.
3. **Growth surprises.** PMIs (HCOB), German data and euro-area GDP.
4. **Sovereign risk premium.** Fiscal stress in a large member (France and Italy are the usual candidates) weighs on the euro and can make the ECB more cautious about tightening.
5. **Inflation persistence.** HICP and core HICP against the 2% target. A hot print helps the euro only if the market believes the ECB will act on it.

## How to read EUR in a pair
EUR is the base of EURUSD, EURGBP, EURJPY, EURCHF and the other euro crosses, so **euro strength is bullish for all of them**. EURX is the euro read on its own, against no single partner.
