# Japanese yen (JPY)

## Central bank: the Bank of Japan
- **Mandate.** Price stability, contributing to the sound development of the economy. A **2% CPI target** has been in place since January 2013. (BoJ, outline of monetary policy)
- **Who decides.** The nine-member **Policy Board**, by majority vote at **eight Monetary Policy Meetings a year**. After each meeting it releases the decision at once, then a Summary of Opinions and, later, the minutes. (BoJ)
- **Instrument.** Since **19 March 2024**, the short-term policy interest rate (the uncollateralised overnight call rate) is the primary tool. That day the Bank **ended negative interest rates and yield curve control**, judging that the 2% target was coming in sight through a virtuous cycle between wages and prices. (BoJ statement, 19 March 2024)
  - *Implication (background):* the BoJ is normalising from near-zero rates, so even small hikes move the yen. The pace is gradual and data-dependent, and wage negotiations (shunto) are a key input.

## Intervention is the Ministry of Finance's call
- The **Minister of Finance** is legally responsible for FX intervention. The BoJ executes it as agent, from the Foreign Exchange Fund Special Account. (BoJ, outline of FX intervention)
- **Most recent episode:** the MoF **sold US dollars and bought yen on 30 April, 4 May and 6 May 2026**, for a total of **¥11,734.9 billion**. (MoF, FX intervention operations, April–June 2026)
  - *Implication (background):* when USDJPY rises fast, intervention risk caps the move. Verbal warnings from MoF officials ("excessive", "speculative" moves) usually come first. Actual intervention is disclosed in monthly and quarterly reports.

## Economy: almost no domestic energy
- Japan's energy self-sufficiency was **15.2% in FY2023 and 16.4% in FY2024**, the lowest in the G7. (METI/ANRE, Japan's Energy 2025)
  - *Implication (background):* dearer oil and LNG widen Japan's trade deficit and weigh on the yen through the terms of trade. This compounds the rate-gap channel when both point the same way.

## Channels that move the yen (background)
1. **The US–Japan yield gap.** The yen is the classic funding currency: a wide gap supports carry trades (short yen), and a narrowing gap unwinds them, often abruptly.
2. **Risk-off flows.** In global stress the yen usually rises as carry trades are closed and Japanese investors repatriate.
3. **BoJ normalisation signals**, and wage data.
4. **Energy prices**, through the trade balance.
5. **Intervention risk**, as above.

## How to read JPY in a pair
JPY is the quote of USDJPY, EURJPY, GBPJPY, AUDJPY and the other yen crosses, so **yen strength is bearish for all of them**.
