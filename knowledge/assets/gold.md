# Gold and precious metals

## How the board reads them
- Gold, silver and platinum read the **US economy**, with the growth and jobs sign **inverted**: a strong US print is **bearish** for the metal, because the metal is the haven trade. Silver and platinum are scored exactly like gold. Measured against A1's board, treating silver as an industrial metal flipped the sign on eight of its cells. (internal: symbols config)
- COT counts both the weekly change and net positioning: a long share above 55% scores +1, below 45% scores −1.

## Channels (background — general market structure, not today's data)
1. **Real yields.** Gold pays no interest, so higher real yields (nominal yields minus expected inflation) raise the cost of holding it. Read the US 10-year and the Fed path in the dossier. A Fed hiking into falling inflation is the classic headwind.
2. **The dollar.** Gold is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar usually weighs on it. DXY is in the cross-asset section.
3. **Haven demand.** Geopolitical stress and equity sell-offs can lift gold even when yields rise. When gold rallies against higher yields, the haven or official-sector bid is dominating.
4. **Official-sector buying.** Central bank purchases have been a structural support in recent years. No such series is in our feeds, so never cite a number for it.
5. **Silver and platinum** also carry an industrial-demand component that the board deliberately ignores. Say so when industrial news (solar, autos) is driving them.

## Reading it with the Fed
The Fed's policy rate and its statement matter more for gold than any single US print. The Fed's own description of transmission names **asset prices and exchange rates** as channels of policy. (Fed, goals of monetary policy)
